Electric vehicles have moved from novelty to default. What began as a niche
choice for early adopters now shapes how manufacturers plan a decade ahead,
how cities budget for infrastructure, and how ordinary drivers weigh up their
next car.

## What changed

Three things arrived at once: batteries got cheaper, charging got faster, and
the driving experience stopped asking for compromise. A car that is quieter,
quicker off the line and cheaper to run is an easy argument to make.

## What comes next

The interesting work is no longer the powertrain. It is everything around it —
the way a car talks to a grid, schedules its own charging, and fits into a
week that does not revolve around refuelling.
